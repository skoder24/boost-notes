# Beginner Boost 2026 learning plan

This 12-week plan turns rwxrob's Beginner Boost track into a practical sequence for exploring a tech career, building a working lab, learning durable foundations, and completing a small project. The timeline is a suggested pace, not an official course schedule; adjust the workload to fit your time and access to equipment.

## How to use the plan

Set aside about five hours a week if you can: use one session to learn, one to practice, and one to review or share what you made. If you have less time, stretch each week into two; prioritize regular hands-on practice over catching up.

Keep a learning journal with the problem you tried, commands or concepts you learned, what failed, how you checked the result, and what you want to learn next. Use AI to research, explain, and review, but verify its suggestions and make sure you can explain the work you keep.

Each week has a checkpoint. Move on when you can demonstrate it in your own words or work, not just when you have finished reading.

## Weeks 1–2: Explore and choose a direction

### Week 1: Know yourself

Start with [Know thyself](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/beginner/know-thyself.md). Reflect on what you enjoy, what others recognize as strengths, what work conditions suit you, and what tasks or constraints you want to avoid. Consider whether you have reliable time, a safe place, and computer access for learning, and make a realistic plan around any gaps.

**Checkpoint:** Write a one-page reflection naming three strengths, three interests, relevant constraints, and questions you want answered before investing in a career path.

### Week 2: Research real work

Read [Getting into tech with AI](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/beginner/getting-into-tech-with-ai.md) and browse the [Beginner Boost guide](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/beginner/README.md). Pick two or three possible roles and investigate current job descriptions, day-to-day responsibilities, and the skills they actually request. Use AI and web search to gather leads, then verify claims against job postings and conversations with people doing the work.

**Checkpoint:** Choose one direction to explore for the next few weeks and list the evidence that led you to it; treat it as a working choice that you can revise.

## Weeks 3–5: Build your working environment

### Week 3: Set up your lab

Follow the Boost's [software installation](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/beginner/install-software.md) and [terminal introduction](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/beginner/open-a-terminal.md). Open a terminal, navigate directories, inspect files, and learn how to find help for a command. Keep a note of your operating system and the tools you installed.

**Checkpoint:** Open a terminal independently, move between folders, list and read files, and explain where your project files are stored.

### Week 4: Learn Git and GitHub by doing

Work through the [Git basics notes](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/beginner/git-basics-notes.md) and practice the [mandatory commands](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/beginner/mandatory-commands.md). Create a small Git repository, make a change, inspect the difference, commit it, and push it to a GitHub repository. Practice checking status before and after each change.

**Checkpoint:** Show a repository with at least three meaningful commits and explain what the working tree, commit, branch, and remote each represent.

For hands-on project ideas centered on Terminal, Git, GitHub, Copilot, and tmux, see [Beginner projects for Terminal, Git, GitHub, and tmux](beginner-projects-terminal-git-github-tmux.md).

### Week 5: Read and write technical material

Use Markdown to document your setup and notes. Practice reading an unfamiliar project by looking at its README, directory structure, and a few files before trying to change anything. The Boost's [code and terminal reading guide](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/beginner/read-code-and-terminal.md) is a useful companion.

**Checkpoint:** Add a clear README to your repository with its purpose, setup instructions, and one thing you learned from reading existing code.

## Weeks 6–8: Learn foundations and work with AI

### Week 6: Choose a small set of relevant skills

Review [Common technologies relevant to most tech jobs](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/beginner/relevant-tech.md). Practice shell fundamentals and choose one additional coding or web skill that fits your selected direction; do not try to learn every language at once. Use the Boost's [just-in-time learning](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/beginner/jit-learning.md) approach to answer questions as they arise.

**Checkpoint:** Create a small script, web page, or other role-relevant artifact and explain what inputs it accepts and what it produces.

### Week 7: Use AI as a tutor and reviewer

Read [Boost tools then and now](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/beginner/boost-tools-then-and-now.md). Ask an AI tool to explain unfamiliar commands or propose a small change; review the proposed steps before running them, inspect the resulting diff, and test the behavior yourself. Keep examples and explanations in your journal using a [knowledge-management](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/beginner/knowledge-management.md) habit.

**Checkpoint:** Complete one small AI-assisted change, identify at least one assumption in the AI's response, and show how you independently verified the result.

### Week 8: Connect the pieces

Learn basic networking and web concepts relevant to your direction, such as clients and servers, DNS, HTTP, and logs. Build or improve a simple static page using Markdown or HTML and publish it if you are comfortable doing so. Focus on understanding how a request reaches a service and how to investigate a failure.

**Checkpoint:** Explain the path from entering a web address to receiving a page, and document one issue you diagnosed while building or publishing your page.

## Weeks 9–10: Build and verify a project

### Week 9: Explore an isolated environment

Learn the difference between a virtual machine and a container, then use one beginner-appropriate lab from the [Boost repository](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/tree/main/docs/shared). Follow setup and cleanup instructions carefully; avoid putting credentials or personal data in a public project.

**Checkpoint:** Run a small test workload in the environment, describe what is isolated from your host, and record how you stopped and removed the lab resources.

### Week 10: Make a useful capstone

Choose a small problem connected to your interests or target role, such as a static resource guide, a command-line helper, or a simple data report. Create a GitHub issue or task list, build in small commits, use AI only with review, and test the result from a clean starting point. Prefer a finished, understandable project over a large feature set.

**Checkpoint:** Share a repository with a clear README, working instructions, meaningful commit history, and evidence that the main behavior works.

## Weeks 11–12: Share your work and plan what comes next

### Week 11: Get feedback and build professional context

Ask a peer, mentor, community, or practitioner for feedback on your project and the role you are exploring. Compare your current skills with real job descriptions, identify gaps, and improve your project's documentation. The Boost's [conferences and job fairs guide](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/blob/main/docs/shared/conferences-and-job-fairs.md) can help you find ways to talk with practitioners.

**Checkpoint:** Record feedback you received, what you changed in response, and three specific skills or experiences that appear repeatedly in relevant roles.

### Week 12: Choose the next learning path

Review your journal and project, then decide whether to deepen your current specialty, explore another role, or continue through the [Beginner Boost track](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/tree/main/docs/beginner). If your foundations feel solid, browse the [Advanced track](https://github.com/rwxrob/boost/tree/main/docs/advanced) for topics such as MCP, agent workflows, and CI/CD; treat these as next steps rather than prerequisites for getting started.

**Checkpoint:** Write a 90-day plan with one target skill, one project milestone, a weekly time commitment, and a way to get feedback.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] I can use a terminal to navigate, inspect files, and find command help.
- [ ] I can make and explain Git commits and publish a repository on GitHub.
- [ ] I can read project documentation, write Markdown, and explain a small piece of code or a script.
- [ ] I can use AI to accelerate learning while checking its claims and reviewing its output.
- [ ] I completed and documented a small project that another person can try.
- [ ] I have a next-step plan based on my interests, constraints, and evidence about real work.
